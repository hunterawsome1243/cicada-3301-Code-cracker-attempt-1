# The first twelve weeks

## The first twelve weeks: instrument first, then the parked lane, then the scorer nobody has

The prompts choose lanes by budget share, but Hunter writes `queue.json`, so the order of the first rounds matters. The doctrine's R1 is the constraint: "No round may open a new key-space sweep until its decoder and adjudicator have a measured power envelope for that round's hypothesis class — measured by planting the correct key and reading the score — never by reasoning about the design" ([ARMADA-DOCTRINE §2](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/master/liber-primus/ARMADA-DOCTRINE.md)). That makes the spec's Phases 1 and 2 (reproduction of the nine solved sections, the stats fingerprint, the rune-native scorer and its power table) the whole of the first month's instrument budget and most of its bounded-lane budget too, which is fine: the power table itself is publishable, since no project has measured a scorer's power on Latin, vowel-dropped English and the Cicada register side by side, and a Latin power near zero becomes the standing conditional on every later negative. The first bounded lane after that is the one Dukotah's handoff ranks as cheapest: resume the Py2.7-MT `exact` lane from its committed 44.14 % cursor with a passing planted-seed control, which also exercises the checkpoint/resume path the overnight loop depends on ([PARKED-SWEEPS.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/master/liber-primus/handoff/PARKED-SWEEPS.md)). The red-team share in those weeks should re-derive the project's own fingerprint numbers (86 doublets, IoC 1.000, entropy 4.857) with independently written code, since Dukotah's L7 red-team found three FOUND-ERROR verdicts by doing exactly that against its own instrument and bars ([round18 L7 RESULTS](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/master/liber-primus/analysis/round18/L7-redteam/RESULTS.md)).

Months two and three are where the GPU and the loop earn their keep. The unswept 32-bit generators (PHP `mt_rand`, .NET, ISAAC, BBS, LFSRs, xorshift, RC4 short keys, KISS), each validated against its reference implementation and run with and without the anti-repeat filter model, are enumerable spaces with a stated fraction, the doctrine's second-best object class; the derived-key lanes B-04/B-05 are the lane the D3 audit found had been foreclosed by the retracted "unsolvable" headline and are still `never-run` ([round12/D3/RESULTS.md](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/master/liber-primus/analysis/round12/D3/RESULTS.md)). The highest-leverage small project remains the P-8 doublet-site audit, a finite human-checkable object with a pre-registered bar ("≥20 sites where an independent count exceeds canon's by exactly one... ⇒ the engineered-filter model is falsified"), which costs days, needs no GPU, and would reopen or close the central structural hypothesis either way ([PARKED.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/master/liber-primus/handoff/PARKED.md)). The one open instrument task, the clock-variant Quagmire-dodge scorer that murphy left unbuilt ("a beam decode over skip forks inside a base_0 search, with its own planted controls... Several days, uncertain to work... Hope 2/10"), is exactly the kind of bounded engineering problem with a built-in control that a loop handles well, and it belongs in the instrument budget of month three, after the rune-native scorer exists to build on ([REPORT_ROUND2.md §4–6](https://github.com/murphyaidanbusiness-ux/liberprimus/blob/main/REPORT_ROUND2.md)). Throughout, the Z340 lesson applies to the transform-screener lane: screen a huge transform space with a substitution-invariant statistic against two nulls (shuffles of the real text and synthetic ciphers of the hypothesised family), fix the feature set before screening to avoid the look-elsewhere effect, and run the expensive solver only on survivors; the Z340 team cut "billions" of schemes to 655,088 solver runs this way and still submitted the result to the FBI for independent verification before publishing ([arXiv 2403.17350](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17350)).
